🧠 Overview

ECE109 introduced the fundamentals of how materials behave in electronic systems, focusing on the relationship between atomic structure and electrical/optical properties.

The course started with basic quantum mechanics, where we learned how electrons behave as waves and occupy discrete energy levels. Concepts like potential wells and tunneling helped build the foundation for understanding how electrons exist inside materials.

We then moved into atomic bonding and crystal structures, which explain why different materials, such as metals, semiconductors, and insulators, have different properties. Learning about crystalline defects was especially interesting, since imperfections in materials can significantly impact performance.

A major part of the course focused on band theory and semiconductors, which are essential for electrical and computer engineering. Understanding how energy bands form and how electrons move within them gave me insight into how devices like diodes and transistors work at a physical level.

The course also covered optical properties, such as how light interacts with materials, and dielectric materials, which are important for capacitors and insulation. Finally, we looked at metallic conduction using models like the free electron model.

Overall, ECE109 helped connect physics and chemistry concepts to real engineering applications, especially in electronics and materials used in hardware systems.
